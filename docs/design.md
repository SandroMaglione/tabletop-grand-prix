# Tabletop Grand Prix

A solo time-attack racer made to show PixiJS 3D (`@pixi/3d`) with a game built on `@typeonce/ritmo`.

## The game

- The whole track is on screen, always. One fixed camera, from above and a little to the side.
- You drive one car. Every attempt starts standing just behind the start line; the clock starts when the car first moves and stops when it crosses the line after every checkpoint, in order.
- You cannot leave the road: both edges are walls. The car slides along a wall and loses speed; it never stops dead.
- Jumps are the one place without walls. Land on the jump's own landing and carry on; come down anywhere else, the road under the jump included, and the attempt starts again from the line. `R` restarts at once.
- The goal is the fastest lap. A ghost of your best lap drives with you, three sectors show where you gained or lost, and three medal times give targets.
- When a lap is done the brand wipes across the whole window, the way a race broadcast cuts away, and behind it the page cuts to the replay: the lap drives itself again on the map, over and over, with the leaderboard beside it. A lap that moved the player up shows it: once the wipe has left, their row slides up to its new place, lit, with the places it gained. `R` starts again at once from any moment of this, and the clock starts when the car moves.
- The leaderboard is up by default, so nobody needs a key to find it: on arriving, on the line and beside the replay. It gets out of the way by itself only while a lap runs. The player closes it with its cross, `L` or `Esc`, and it then stays closed, on later visits too, until they open it again.

## Driving

- One way to steer, and the arrows are the game: up is gas, left and right turn the wheel, down or space brakes; on a gamepad the right trigger, the left trigger and the stick. Nothing on screen explains it. When the car has waited on the line for five seconds with nothing pressed, the arrow keys show in the middle of the map with the one that goes lit. (The simulation and the replay format still know a second way, pointing where to go, which the game no longer offers.)
- The car forgives: a turn that is held slows the car by itself, so holding the throttle through a corner settles at a speed the corner can take instead of running wide into the wall. Lifting or braking before the corner and a clean line are still faster.
- The car drifts. In a turn, gas and brake held together (up with down or space; on a gamepad the right and left triggers) slide it round on an arc of its own, exactly: about 12 m of radius with the wheel let go, down to 7.5 m with it held toward the turn, out to 26 m with it held away, the nose pointing inside the way it goes. The slide keeps more speed than gripping round does, and letting the brake go straightens the car and pushes it forward, more the longer the slide was held (nothing under a third of a second, the whole push at 1.3 s). A wall, a jump or too little speed ends it with nothing. With the gas down the brake alone only drags; to brake hard, lift the gas. A lap with the tight corners drifted is about two and a half seconds faster than a clean one without, and gold asks for it.
- Nothing on screen explains the drift either, but the speed panel always carries it: its name, the two pedals in the keys of the device in use, and a bar that fills as a slide earns its push. Sparks fly off the rear wheels while it does, blue and then orange when the push is whole.
- The boost pad before the jump is strong enough that a car driven flat out through the spiral clears the gap; a wall hit on the way out of the spiral can still leave it short.

## The track's features

| feature | rule |
|---|---|
| Jump over a lower part of the track | A ramp, a gap with no road and no walls, a landing. Too slow and you fall short: reset. |
| Spiral climb | The road climbs around a full turn and passes over itself on a bridge deck. |
| Banked turn | The surface tilts into the corner; the car holds more speed. |
| Crate wall | Crates stacked two high block most of the road, with barrels behind. The car shoves them: they slide, spin and fall off each other, and stay where they stop. Ploughing through costs speed by how much it moves; there is a gap to thread. |
| Slalom | Three slanted fences of fixed posts, each closing one side of the road in turn. A post never moves; hitting one costs speed and turns the car round it. |
| Balls | Heavy balls standing in the road, with a lane between them. A ball that is hit rolls a long way, off walls and into whatever is ahead. |
| Swing doors | Two leaves hinged at the walls meet in the middle. The car pushes them open; near the free ends they cost little, near a hinge they are almost a wall. They stay open. |
| Boost pad | Pushes the car while it is on it. One sits before the jump. |
| Ford | A pool over the right two thirds of the start straight. Water holds the car back while its centre is in it; the dry lane on the left is the tight line into the first corner. |
| Meadow | Long grass over the inside three quarters of the wide sweeper. It holds the car back a little: through it is the short way round and costs some speed, and a lane stays clear along the outside wall. |
| Stony ground | Rough ground through the esses, still road, with eight rocks jutting out of it, low enough to drive over. A car that rolls onto one at speed is thrown up and comes down a few metres on: for that moment it has no engine, no brakes and little steering, and the knock costs a little speed. Nothing stops at a rock; there is room to weave between them. |

Nothing on the track runs on a timer: every obstacle is where the player left it, so a lap is only as good as the driving. Everything the car knocked about goes back when the attempt restarts.

A surface is a patch of the road in road coordinates (`TrackData.surfaces`): the rules read only which patch is under the car's centre, and hold the car back by a share of its speed (`grassDrag`, `waterDrag` in `tuning.ts`). A rock is a `Bump`: a circle that throws the car when it rolls onto it (`bumpKick`, `bumpLoss`). The splash, the lane through the grass, the wet tyre tracks and the rattle on the stones are the page's.

## Sharing laps

- A lap is kept as its inputs (a replay), not as positions: the simulation gives the same lap from the same inputs on every device, so a replay is the best lap on the device, what is sent to the leaderboard, and what a ghost is driven from.
- The leaderboard is one board per track and rules version, each player's best run only. The server drives every replay it is sent and files the time it gets, so a time on the board was driven.
- A player has no account, and never has to type a name: with the first finished lap the device is registered under a made-up one and the lap goes to the board under it. A made-up name is three short words about an animal at the wheel, how it is, what it is and what it does ("Shy Otter Skids", "Late Goose Revs"; `src/host/handles.ts`, close to two hundred thousand of them), and the player can change it from the board, where `RANDOM` draws another. The device keeps a secret that proves later requests are its own. A name belongs to one device. Names that read the same are the same name, and some are refused (see `docs/server.md`).
- The ghost beside the car is, by default, the next closest run: the slowest one still faster than the player's best. The player can race the fastest run instead (by lap, or in the sector the board is ordered by), one driver per sector (the fastest in each unless they pick another; at each line the ghost becomes the next sector's driver, from beside the car, so each sector is raced from level), their own best, or none; each choice races that ghost alone (none: nothing at all). Then a click on any run of the board races its ghost too, or stops racing it, the chosen one included: a selection by hand on top of the choice.
- The board can be ordered by the lap or by one sector. From a board by sector, watching a run shows only that sector, again and again.
- With no server the game plays the same, with the device's own best as the ghost. A lap that could not be sent is kept and sent later.

## Scale

- Units are metres. The car is about 2.4 long and 1.3 wide. The road is about 6.5 wide.
- The track fits a footprint of about 100 by 60 so the car is roughly 2.5% of the screen's width.
- Top speed is about 30 m/s; a lap is 20 to 30 seconds.
- The simulation runs at 120 ticks per second. The finish time is exact to the line crossing inside the tick and shown to a thousandth.

## The look

A solid scale model on a pale table, not a flat map.

- The terrain is one thick slab with grass on top. The road is **carved into it**: a channel with real side faces of packed earth, so every edge of the track reads as depth. Those faces are the walls.
- Ramps, embankments and the spiral are **solid masses** that rise out of the slab, with the road's channel continuing on top. A jump's ramp ends in a cliff. A bridge is a deck on pillars with the lower road visible beneath.
- Real materials and daylight: matte asphalt with painted lines, red and white kerbs, concrete, wooden crates. A sun that casts soft shadows (the car's shadow in the air shows where it will land), ambient occlusion in the channels, a light tilt-shift.
- An ink outline on the car and on what can be hit, nothing else.
- The map is the picture: the camera turns a little (6 to 20 degrees) to whatever shows the board largest in the window. The HUD is a few small dark panels the map shows through, over the board's corners, drawn like a racing game's timing screens: a pale glassy rim, a hatched header, rows as bars, a state as a hatched band of colour, white text and one yellow accent. The timing panel is top left: its header is the Typeonce plate (the maker's pixel logo and "more games" with a pixel arrow, a link to typeonce.games that eases to a brighter state under the pointer), then the clock, the best lap, the ghost, the sectors as tabs and the gap to the ghost on a green or red band. The speed is bottom right, messages and the result bottom centre, the leaderboard top right as a table with the player's row lit. After a finished lap the brand wipes across the whole window (three slanted slabs, yellow, white, then the dark one that covers, and the lockup on a band that comes in fast, creeps on across the middle and leaves fast to the left with the slabs); behind it the replay has started, with the board open beside it. The wipe waits a moment and a half after the finish, so the player sees the lap is done first. While the replay plays the window wears a border of recorded footage (`replay-look.ts`): along its edges only, a darker band with scan lines inside a thin pale frame, the map itself untouched, and `REPLAY` top centre. The timing panel is away for the replay (the lap's time is on the result), so the leaderboard carries the Typeonce plate as its first row, with room round it. From the second lap of a visit, the replay of the player's own lap carries a small email sign-up in the timing panel's empty place, once the wipe has left ("an email each time a new game ships", a field, "get new games", and a cross in its corner): it comes in once with a short move, a lit rim and a glint (it simply appears with less motion asked for), never takes the keyboard by itself, goes with the replay, and never comes back on the device once the player has signed up or closed it. Off typeonce.games it answers itself and sends nothing. The start prompt shows the arrow keys as raised keys, the one that goes being pressed. Bottom left, small, "made with PixiJS 3D" with PixiJS's own logo and the renderer in use, a link to pixijs.com/3d.
- The scene is drawn on WebGPU where the browser's GPU can run PixiJS 3D on it, and on WebGL elsewhere (`src/host/backend.ts`, as Inkline Town does it: the adapter must have the feature PixiJS 3D needs and must really draw a frame). `?renderer=webgl` asks for WebGL. Every shader bit and effect carries its code twice, `gpu` in WGSL and `gl` in GLSL; a bit without one half draws nothing of its own on that renderer, with no error, so change both.
- Three patches of the road show what the renderer does: the ford (shader water, a splash and a wake, wet tyre tracks that dry), the meadow (long instanced blades that lean in the wind, are pushed aside by the car as it touches them and spring back up behind it) and the stony ground (rough dirt and gravel, half-buried rocks the car bounces over).
- From the page's first paint until the game has drawn its first frames, a loading screen is up: the dark hatched cover of the lap-end wipe with the Typeonce lockup floating up and down. It is plain markup in `index.html` (the lockup written in by the build, `vite.config.ts`), so it shows before any script has arrived, and it leaves to the left like the wipe (`src/host/loading.ts`). A game that fails before its first frame says so there.
- Colours are in `src/host/palette.ts`. Change them there.

## How the code is laid out

- `src/sim`: the simulation, headless, on ritmo. `game.ts` is its entry: the schedule of one tick and the runtime.
  - `schema.ts`: every value the world keeps, as an Effect schema, so a race can be saved, restored, replayed and fuzzed. A value that only means something in one situation lives in that situation's state (the car on the road or in the air, the boost off, on a pad or fading, the lap ready, running or finished).
  - `state.ts`: what the world holds (saved state, unsaved state, what the host gives). `systems/`: one file per system. `view.ts`: what the session reads.
  - `car.ts` and `obstacles.ts`: the physics, as plain functions. `tuning.ts`: the game's values, each with its unit and range. `track/`: the track data and the queries on it. `types.ts`: the contract the page reads.
  - The player's input reaches the world through one input source, which is also what replays and the fuzzer feed.
- `test/`: a test per rule, `invariants.ts` (what must hold after every tick), `fuzz.test.ts` (random driving on the real schedule against the invariants), `save.test.ts` (a race saved mid-lap restores and plays on the same), `replay.test.ts` (a lap driven again gives the same lap; the kept lap pins the rules).
- `src/host`: the page. `contract.ts` names its parts: `stage.ts` (Pixi app, view, camera), `world/` (terrain, road, scenery, light), `actors/` (car, ghost, crates, pads, gates, particles, skid marks), and `main.ts` with input, HUD, sound and the ghost.
  - `rerun.ts`: the lap just driven, driven again in a second session for the replay after the finish. While it plays, the actors, the scene and the engine sound follow its view; the HUD and the leaderboard still read the player's own race.
  - `brand/`: the Typeonce plate and the wipe, and the "made with PixiJS 3D" note.
- The page reads the simulation only through `src/sim/index.ts`.
- `src/sim/replay.ts`: a lap as its inputs, and `runReplay`, which drives one again. `src/sim/version.ts` is the rules version; raise it whenever a change moves a lap.
- `src/api`: what the game and the leaderboard's server say to each other. `src/server` and `alchemy.run.ts`: the server on Cloudflare.
- `pnpm check` is the whole check: types, lint, the simulation lint and the tests. `pnpm verify:engines` drives the kept replays in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit and compares them with Node, bit for bit.
